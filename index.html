Enraged Behemoths

Monsters at Starting Strength gain +2 OC. Once damaged they gain +1 to Hit, and below half strength +1 to Wound as well. That mostly cancels the "Damaged" penalty (-1 to Hit at 1–5 wounds) and sharpens wounded monsters.

Range and tempo

Charge rolls are 2D6 for a maximum distance, so the chance to reach a target is the chance the roll equals or beats the gap.

Chance to charge from a given gap

Deep Strike arrivals must set up more than 9" away, so a same-turn charge from there needs a 9 or more (28%) if your pack allows it. Check the mission pack.

Practical read: Keep your Synapse monsters tight (coherency 2" to one model, 9" to all), use terrain to block lines for long-range guns, and use Tanglestrike Rounds (-2" Move on a tethered enemy) to slow a charger.

Detachment rules and Stratagems

Crusher Stampede, Talons of the Norn Queen and Singular Purpose are from the Wahapedia 11th edition Tyranids pages (faction pack 1.2).

Game plan

Twelve units and 166 wounds means every unit you lose is a big share of the army. The list wins by holding ground with monsters that are hard to shift and trading only when the trade favours you.

What the list does well

  • Two Norns that can each become OC 15 with Feel No Pain 5+ on a chosen objective.
  • Ten models with 10+ wounds, all getting better at hitting once damaged.
  • Long guns (rupture cannon 48", Exocrines 36") that shoot from cover.
  • Psychic Maleceptors and Torrent acid spray that ignore cover penalties.

Where it is weak

  • Only five cheap models. You cannot screen deep strikers or soak a charge with chaff.
  • Twelve activations. A unit that is badly placed costs a large share of your turn.
  • Bring It Down pays your opponent for every big model he kills.
  • Your home objective relies on one Tyrannofex and five Barbgaunts.

Rules of thumb at the table

  1. Score first, kill second. Decide which objectives you will hold this turn before you pick a single target.
  2. Move as groups. When one monster steps into the open, he can focus all his guns on it. When four step out together, he kills one at most.
  3. Shoot his anti-monster guns first. Lascannons, melta and high-damage cannons decide whether your monsters survive. Elite infantry can wait.
  4. Let melee come to you. Your Norns and Hive Tyrant hit hardest on the counter-charge, after his unit has spent its own turn getting there.
  5. Pull wounded monsters back. A damaged monster still shoots well thanks to Enraged Behemoths, and keeping it alive denies him kill points.

Norn playbook

Both Norns have Singular Purpose, chosen at the start of the first battle round. Talons of the Norn Queen lets each of them re-pick once per battle in your Command phase (Protean Purpose). That makes them two objective anchors early and a hunter late.

The two options

Objective
While within range of that objective marker: OC 15 and Feel No Pain 5+, until the end of the battle.
Enemy unit
Re-roll Hit rolls and Wound rolls for every attack against that unit, until the end of the battle.

The Emissary also has Feel No Pain 4+ against mortal wounds. The Assimilator has Harpoon Barbs: an enemy that falls back from her takes D6 mortal wounds on a 2+.

Opening picks

  • Split them. Two different objectives, never the same one. OC 15 on one point is already more than most units can contest.
  • Assimilator on the hardest point. She keeps it all game. Harpoon Barbs punishes units that tie her up and leave, and Catalytic Biofortification protects her from psychic damage.
  • Emissary on the point another group can take over, or the far point a fast unit would steal. She is the one who re-picks later.

When the Emissary re-picks

Re-pick when all of these are true

  • Another group already controls her objective with OC to spare at the start of your turn.
  • A high-value target is within 18" of her or will be by next turn: a Terminator brick, a Daemon Prince, a heavy gun platform, or a unit on the objective you need.
  • The target will still be alive and relevant next turn, because this choice lasts the rest of the game.
  • The mission pays for it: Punishment, Meatgrinder and Consecrate all reward a late push on his home objective.

Do not re-pick when

  • Her leaving would drop the objective. Most primaries score at the end of your Command phase, and Protean Purpose also happens in your Command phase, so her OC 15 is gone before you score that turn.
  • Nothing worth the re-rolls is in reach. A re-pick onto a three-model squad wastes the only one she has.
  • You are in Destroyer's Wrath and objective count is decided by one point.
The Assimilator stays put in almost every game. She re-picks only if her objective has been removed or the game is decided and a kill is worth more than the point.

Matchup: Chaos Space Marines

For the rematch against Gil's Veterans of the Long War and Cabal of Chaos list. If you play Purge the Foe, his Take and Hold primary is Immovable Object: 3 VP each turn for a central objective and 5 VP per non-home objective. If you both play Take and Hold, you both get Battlefield Dominance, a straight objective fight your OC should win.

Target order

  1. Predator Destructor and Forgefiend: his monster killers. Rupture Tyrannofex first.
  2. Biker squads with meltaguns: acid spray Torrent when they come in range.
  3. Chosen with Fabius Bile: Exocrines and Maleceptors, no invulnerable save to stop AP-3.
  4. Terminators with the Sorcerer: Norns in melee with Lesser Prey, never the first shooting target.
  5. Cultists and Legionaries: only when they sit on an objective you need.

How his plan works against you

  • Focus of Hatred gives his whole army re-rolls against one unit each turn. Give him no lone monster to pick, and if one must be exposed, make it a Norn on her objective with Feel No Pain 5+.
  • He hides until you step out. Your Norns on central objectives deny his 3 VP and 5 VP scores every turn he stays hidden, so the clock works against him.
  • Cabal psychic damage: keep a CP for Catalytic Biofortification on the Assimilator. The Emissary already has Feel No Pain 4+ against mortal wounds.
  • Emissary re-pick: the Terminators or the Daemon Prince once they commit to one of your objectives.

Deployment B: diagonal zones

Mission plans

Your Force Disposition and your opponent's decide your primary. Talons of the Norn Queen gives you Take and Hold and Crusher Stampede gives you Purge the Foe, so you can choose either.

Take and Hold Recommended

Every mission pays per objective and none needs kills. Two Norns at OC 15 and monsters at +2 OC win objective fights, and an opponent who hides loses points every turn he stays hidden. Protean Purpose can also move a Norn's OC 15 onto a new objective you are pushing into.

Purge the Foe

Most missions pay for killing a unit every turn and out-killing your opponent. That is harder with twelve activations against a hiding opponent, and an opponent playing Take and Hold gets Immovable Object against you, a better mission than your Unstoppable Force.

As Take and Hold you get Battlefield Dominance against Take and Hold, Immovable Object against Purge the Foe, Determined Acquisition against Disruption, Purge and Secure against Reconnaissance and Inescapable Dominion against Priority Assets.

Battlefield Dominance Take and Hold vs Take and Hold

  • Rounds 1 and 2, end of your turn: 2 VP if you control more objectives than your opponent.
  • Round 2 onward, end of your Command phase (end of your turn in round 5): 3 VP for each objective you control, including your home.
  • Same timing: +2 VP for each of those objectives other than your home, if you control your home.
  • Home plus two others is 13 VP; home plus three others is 18, capped at 15. Without your home, three objectives are only 9 VP.

How the Norns play it

Your home objective multiplies everything else, so it is the most important point on the table. Both Norns anchor the two centre points all game. Extra objectives above home plus three add nothing, so the Emissary re-picks only onto an enemy unit that threatens your home or one of your three scoring points.

Placement for this mission

  • A: as drawn. The rupture Tyrannofex and Barbgaunts on home are the core of the plan; do not move them forward.
  • B: as drawn. Centre NW is 4" away, so the Tyrant group takes it on turn 1 for the round 1 bonus.
  • C: as drawn. Home and expansion are both yours from turn 1, so you out-number him in round 1 unless he reaches a centre.

Immovable Object Take and Hold vs Purge the Foe

  • Any round, end of your turn: 3 VP if you control one or more central objectives.
  • Rounds 2 to 4, end of your Command phase: 5 VP for each objective you control, excluding your home.
  • Round 5, end of your turn: 5 VP for each objective you control, excluding your home.
  • A centre plus three non-home objectives is 18 VP, capped at 15. Two non-home objectives including a centre already make 13.

How the Norns play it

No kills are needed at all. Both Norns stand on the central objectives, which pay at the end of every turn including round 1. Neither Norn should re-pick unless an objective is removed or a single enemy unit is the only thing contesting one of your points.

Placement for this mission

  • Your home scores nothing. Leave the Barbgaunts there to stop him walking onto it, and use the rupture Tyrannofex elsewhere.
  • A: rupture Tyrannofex joins the expansion group with the acid spray Tyrannofex and Maleceptor, covering the right lane.
  • B: rupture Tyrannofex joins the centre NW group, where it has lanes onto both centres.
  • C: rupture Tyrannofex joins the acid spray Tyrannofex on your expansion, giving that point OC 14.

Determined Acquisition Take and Hold vs Disruption

  • Any round, end of your turn: 2 VP for each non-home objective you control that you did not control at the start of the turn.
  • Round 2 onward, end of your Command phase (end of your turn in round 5): 3 VP for each objective you control, including your home.
  • Same timing: +3 VP for each of those objectives in your opponent's territory.
  • Home, expansion and both centres are 12 VP. Add his expansion and you reach the 15 cap. It is unconfirmed whether the centres on the territory line count as his territory.

How the Norns play it

This is the mission where Protean Purpose pays off best. Both Norns open on the centres. In round 3, the Norn nearest his expansion re-picks it as her new objective: she walks over, takes it for 2 VP, and holds it with OC 15 for 6 VP a turn. The other Norn keeps her centre.

Purge and Secure Take and Hold vs Reconnaissance

  • Any round, end of your turn: 3 VP if a friendly unit within range of an objective destroyed an enemy unit, or if an enemy unit that started the turn on an objective was destroyed.

  • End of your turn: 3 VP if you control a non-home objective you did not control at the start of the turn.
  • Three non-home objectives plus one qualifying kill reach the 15 cap.

How the Norns play it

Norns on objectives that kill in melee score the kill bonus without moving. Gun groups should shoot from in range of an objective so their kills count too. Re-pick the Emissary only onto a unit sitting on one of your objectives, since killing it scores twice over.

Placement for this mission

  • B: as drawn. Keep the Emissary on your expansion; anything fast that comes for it dies on an objective.
  • All maps: do not leave a gun group standing between objectives. Move it a few inches onto a point before shooting.

Inescapable Dominion Take and Hold vs Priority Assets

How the Norns play it

Three objectives every turn from round 1 is worth 13 VP a round. Both Norns anchor so you never drop below three. In round 4 the Emissary re-picks onto the unit holding his home objective, then ends the game standing on it for the extra 5 VP.

Unstoppable Force Purge the Foe vs Take and Hold

  • Any round, end of your turn: 3 VP if you destroyed one or more enemy units.
  • Round 2 onward, end of your Command phase (end of your turn in round 5): 4 VP for each objective you control, excluding your home.
  • Same timing: 3 VP at the end of your turn if you control an objective you did not control at the start of the turn (not your home).
  • End of the battle: 5 VP if you control one or more central objectives. End-of-battle VP are not subject to the 15 VP round cap.
  • Three held non-home objectives (12 VP) plus one kill (3 VP) reach the 15 VP cap every round.

How the Norns play it

Both Norns anchor objectives through round 3. The Emissary may re-pick in round 4 only if her point is held by others with room to spare. The Assimilator never re-picks: she is your 5 VP centre at the end of the battle.

Round by round

RoundPlan

Punishment Purge the Foe vs Disruption

  • Start of your turn: condemn 1 to 3 enemy units that are within range of objectives or destroyed one of yours last turn (one unit if none qualify).
  • Any round, end of a turn: 5 VP if one or more condemned units left the battlefield this turn.
  • Round 2 onward, end of your Command phase (end of your turn in round 5): 4 VP if you control one or more non-home objectives, and 5 VP if you control more objectives than your opponent.
  • End of the battle: 8 VP for controlling your opponent's home objective.

How the Norns play it

Holding three objectives scores the same flat 4 VP as one, so what matters is out-numbering him. Both Norns anchor the two centre points so the comparison goes your way. The Emissary is the late-game hunter: re-pick her in round 4 onto the unit holding his home objective, kill it, and stand there in round 5 for the 8 VP.

Round by round

RoundPlan
1Condemn units you can actually kill this turn. One dead condemned unit is 5 VP, the most valuable single kill in this mission.
2Hold both centre points plus your expansion. Condemn up to three; aim at least one shooting group at one of them.
3Keep condemning the cheapest unit sitting on an objective. Cultists or a small biker squad are easy 5 VP.
4Emissary re-picks onto the unit on or nearest his home and moves toward it. The Tyrant group takes over her centre point.
5Emissary ends the game on his home objective for 8 VP. Everyone else just keeps the objective count ahead.

Meatgrinder Purge the Foe vs Purge the Foe

  • Any round, end of your turn: 3 VP if you destroyed one or more enemy units.
  • Round 2 onward, end of your Command phase (end of your turn in round 5): 4 VP if you control one or more non-home objectives.
  • Round 2 onward, end of your turn: 5 VP if more enemy units were destroyed this turn than friendly units were destroyed in his previous turn.
  • Same timing: 5 VP if you control your opponent's home objective.

How the Norns play it

Only one non-home objective is needed, so the Assimilator holding it with OC 15 covers the 4 VP alone. That frees the Emissary to re-pick early, as soon as round 2 or 3, onto the unit that threatens your kill count, then push for his home objective, which pays 5 VP every turn you hold it.

Round by round

RoundPlan
1Kill one unit for 3 VP and lose as little as possible: the out-kill check compares against what he kills next turn.
2Assimilator on one objective. Pick cheap units to kill (screens, small squads) to win the unit count, not the points.
3Emissary re-picks onto the biggest threat near his home and starts walking. Every unit you keep alive makes the next out-kill check easier.
4Emissary on or near his home: 5 VP per turn there. Keep the gun groups behind cover until they can shoot.

Consecrate Purge the Foe vs Reconnaissance

  • Rule: a friendly unit that destroys a unit becomes a consecration unit. At the end of your turn it can consecrate one non-home objective it is in range of, placing your operation marker there.
  • Any round, end of your turn: 3 VP if one or two objectives are consecrated, or 6 VP if three or more are.
  • Round 2 onward, end of your Command phase (end of your turn in round 5): 4 VP for one or more non-home objectives, and 4 VP for controlling more objectives than your opponent.
  • End of the battle: 5 VP if your opponent's home objective is consecrated.

How the Norns play it

The Norns are your best consecrators: they stand on objectives with OC 15 and kill things in melee, so a kill and the consecration happen on the same point. Gun groups only consecrate if the shooting unit itself ends the turn in range, so park at least one Exocrine on each objective you want consecrated.

Round by round

RoundPlan
1Kill something with a unit that will be in range of an objective at the end of your turn.
2Aim for two consecrated objectives (3 VP) plus the two objective-control scores (8 VP).
3Get to three consecrated (6 VP every turn from here on). Prioritise kills by units already on unconsecrated points.
4Emissary re-picks and hunts toward his home: a kill there in round 5 consecrates it for 5 VP at the end.
5Emissary kills a unit and ends in range of his home objective. Everyone else keeps the objective count ahead.

Destroyer's Wrath Purge the Foe vs Priority Assets

  • Any round, end of your turn: 3 VP if you destroyed one or more enemy units.
  • Round 2 onward, end of your Command phase (end of your turn in round 5): 4 VP for one or more non-home objectives, and 6 VP for controlling more objectives than your opponent.
  • Round 2 onward, end of your turn: 4 VP if you destroyed more enemy units this turn than he destroyed of yours in his previous turn.
  • No end-of-battle scoring. A full turn is 17 VP, so the 15 VP cap lets you miss one trigger.

How the Norns play it

Objective count is worth 6 VP a turn, so both Norns stay on objectives all game. Re-pick only to kill a specific unit that is contesting one of your points, and only if another unit can keep that point while she fights.

Round by round

RoundPlan
1Kill one unit for 3 VP. Keep expensive monsters out of his sightlines.
2Hold more objectives than him: both centres plus your expansion. Kill a cheap unit to win the out-kill check.
3Hold and trade. Pull wounded monsters back so he cannot kill units for free.
4If one objective is being swarmed, that Norn may re-pick onto the swarming unit, as long as another unit keeps her point.
5Primaries score at the end of your turn. Make sure you out-number his objectives after your last moves.
